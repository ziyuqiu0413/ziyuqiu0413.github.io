Current Project 現行研究

Silverstealers: Craftsmen, Technology, and State in Qing China

A.M. thesis · Harvard University · Advisor Mark C. Elliott

The monetization of silver made it part of everyday social and economic life in late imperial China. In its daily use as currency, the materiality of silver — its weight, purity, fineness, and form — supplied the criteria by which its monetary value was judged. Knowing silver was therefore a condition of competent participation in economic life. It was also, for those who knew it well enough, an immensely lucrative opportunity: counterfeiting silver from other metals grew into a substantial business, and as its techniques proliferated across the empire the Qing state sought to curb this “misapplication” of technology through legal reform and revision of the official silver system.

This project traces the interplay between the spread of knowledge, technological practice, and the presence of the state in the making of fake silver. It draws on two bodies of evidence. The first is the “silver handbooks” 銀譜 produced in Ming–Qing marketplaces, which show how vernacular knowledge about silver was recorded, circulated, and put to work. The second is the abundant record of counterfeiting cases in Qing legal archives. Chinese and Manchu materials together show such cases arising in every corner of the empire — from Jiangnan to Tibet, from official silver vaults to the marketplace outside Beijing’s Chaoyang Gate — and their handling reveals how the Qing state came to comprehend technical knowledge from below, and to act against its dangers.

Earlier Work 前期研究

Producing Textiles for the Frontier: The Official Textile Trade between Jiangnan and Xinjiang in the Qing Era

Undergraduate thesis · Duke Kunshan University · Advisors Andrew Field and Lei Lin

A study of the official textile trade that carried Jiangnan silk to the Qing northwest frontier between 1757 and 1853 — the workshops that produced the cloth, the administration that moved it, and the frontier economy that received it. Presented at the Harvard East Asian Society Annual Conference and, in an earlier form, at the DKU Undergraduate Humanities Research Conference, where it received the Best Paper Award.
